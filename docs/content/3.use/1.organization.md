Alexandrie separates content organisation from access rights. This lets personal spaces, projects, and team documentation coexist on one instance.

| Item      | Use                                                    |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Team      | Groups people around an organisation or collective.    |
| Workspace | Defines a scope for content and collaboration.         |
| Category  | Classifies workspace documents by topic or activity.   |
| Document  | Holds Markdown, files, and optionally child documents. |
| Resource  | A file uploaded to the instance.                       |

Even if this items are different by nature, they are all nodes and share exactly the same properties and capabilities. They are only different by their apparence in the application.

So the hierarchy is flexible: a document can live under a workspace or a category, and the structure can evolve without rewriting content. You can have multiple-nested categories, documents with children, and workspaces with different types of content.

Note that the hierarchy apply properties and permissions down the tree. If you have read access to a workspace, you can read all its categories and documents. You can also add more granular permissions to a specific document or category, which overrides the inherited access.  
Also note that if you delete a node, all of its children will be deleted as well. So keeping a clean hierarchy will help manage easily your content.

## Command Center

Even if you have a clean hierarchy, as long as you have a lot of content, it can be hard to find what you are looking for. The command center is a powerful tool to navigate and manage your content.

![command center](/assets/command-center.png)

You can open the command center by pressing `Ctrl + K` (or `Cmd + K` on Mac) or `/`. You can search for any node by name, and you can also use commands to create, move, or delete nodes.

With the command center, you can quickly navigate across the application. You can also use the advanced tab to filter your search or perform full-text search.

## Teams

Teams are special workspaces that group people around an organisation or collective. They can contain workspaces, categories, and documents. They are useful to manage access for a group of people rather than individuals.

Teams have also special tools to manage members and permissions, display statistics, and configure the instance. They are also the only nodes that can be shared with external users.
